No lever press is needed here: where R-01's head points is where the marker goes. Its network is told which of the four sides of its cell are open, the direction and rough distance to the cheese, its own speed and the last few cells it visited, as numbers. It never sees the whole map, and this view is our drawing of its simulation, not a picture it sees. Escaping earns a reward; every step, every bump into a wall and every dead end counts against it, and a maze that runs out of time ends without the cheese. Training starts with small open mazes and grows them as the rat improves. Each maze escaped raises this hour's escape rate and each one that runs out of time lowers it; on the hour, the rate sizes the $LABRAT burn below.

Rat burns

Simulated Test stream loading

One $LABRAT burn an hour, sized by how well R-01 ran the maze that hour: 5% of the wallet's LABRAT × escape rate. Escapes and timeouts are counted through the hour (escape rate = escapes ÷ both); on the hour, the rate sets the burn, and one transaction sends it out of circulation. The burn is never more than 5% of what the wallet holds in an hour, and an hour without an escape burns nothing. One burn an hour, instead of one per maze, keeps gas low.

    How the burn works

    R-01 doesn't know what a token is. It runs a maze, and a fixed rule in the code turns how well it runs each hour into one $LABRAT burn from the rat's own wallet.

    1. It runs the maze liveR-01 plays Rat Maze while it trains: its head steers a marker through the corridors to the cheese at the exit. You can watch it above.
    2. Escapes and timeouts are counted each hourReaching the cheese is an escape. A maze that runs out of time is a timeout. The hour's escape rate is escapes ÷ (escapes + timeouts).
    3. On the hour, the escape rate sets the burn5% of the LABRAT in the rat's wallet at the hour's close × that hour's escape rate, rounded down. Never more than 5% in an hour; no escapes, no burn.
    4. One transaction sends it out of circulationThe same signer that sends the buybacks sends the burn, one an hour, through the token's own burn (so the supply falls) or, where a token has none, to the dead address. It is checked before it is signed.
    5. Verified on chain before it countsA burn appears above only once its transaction is confirmed, with a link to it on the explorer. Until the rule is switched on, every burn is simulated: checked on the live chain, never sent.

    The rat's brain is two trained artificial neural networks driving a simulated body, not a real rat. The same brain launched $LABRAT, plays for the buybacks on the buyback page, and its fingerprint is on-chain.
